# Direct Motion From One Segment

## Interactive figure

[Open the full interactive Bokeh figure](../assets/plots/direct_segment_motion.html){ .md-button .md-button--primary target="_blank" }

Drag the endpoints \(A\) and \(B\), or use the sliders to change the direct
motion.  The target segment \(A'B'\) is generated by

\[
M(z)=e^{i\theta}z+v,
\]

so the two segments always have the same length.  Set \(\theta=0\) on the
angle slider to see the translation case.  The back-rotation slider rotates the
image segment backward about \(O\), keeping dotted radii to the moving
endpoints; at 100 percent it lands on the preimage segment.

## What this visualisation shows

Needham classifies direct motions as either translations or rotations.  In this
figure, \(\theta=0\) gives a translation and the orange vector shows the shift
from \(A\) to \(A'\).  When \(\theta\ne0\), the same formula has a fixed point
\(O\), and the construction shows the center, two radii, the rotation arc, and
an optional backward rotation of the image.

The segment \(AB\) alone does not determine a unique direct motion, but once
the angle and translation are chosen the image segment is forced.  This makes
the two cases in the classification visible while keeping the equal-length
condition automatic.
